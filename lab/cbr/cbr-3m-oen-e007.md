### Preregistered Experimental Specification — Draft v1.0

## 1. Experiment Identity

**Experiment ID:** CBR-E007  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Title:** Persistent Computational Structures and Emergent Metric Distortion  
**Protocol:** CBR-OEN Experimental Protocol v1.0

# 2. Scientific Question

CBR-E007 asks:

> **When a persistent computational structure exists within an emergent metric substrate, does its presence systematically modify local computational transition costs and therefore distort the effective metric around it?**

The experiment introduces no:

- mass;
- gravitational field;
- curvature target;
- attraction;
- force law;
- Newtonian potential;
- Einstein equation.

Instead, it asks whether persistent computation itself can produce a measurable local change in computational accessibility.

The conceptual sequence is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{persistent computation}
\rightarrow
\text{cost redistribution}
\rightarrow
\text{metric distortion}
}
\]

---

# 3. Core Hypothesis

### CBR-H007

Persistent computational structures can locally alter the transition-cost field of a relational substrate, producing a reproducible distortion in the emergent metric relative to matched structure-free controls.

Let the substrate be:

\[
(K_t,C_t)
\]

where:

- \(K_t\) is the relational/simplicial state;
- \(C_t\) is the local computational cost field.

Introduce persistent structure:

\[
P.
\]

Then the hypothesis predicts:

\[
C_t
\rightarrow
C_t^{(P)}
\]

and consequently:

\[
d_C(A,B)
\rightarrow
d_C^{(P)}(A,B).
\]

The metric change is:

\[
\Delta d_P(A,B)
=
d_C^{(P)}(A,B)
-
d_C^{(0)}(A,B).
\]

---

# 4. Null Hypothesis

The null hypothesis is:

> Persistent computational structures produce no reproducible metric distortion beyond generic changes caused by equivalent computational activity, local complexity, graph occupancy, or random cost perturbations.

Thus the experiment must distinguish:

\[
\text{effect of persistence}
\]

from:

\[
\text{effect of simply doing more computation}.
\]

---

# 5. Why E007 Matters

CBR-E006 establishes a candidate relationship:

\[
\text{computational cost}
\rightarrow
\text{effective distance}.
\]

CBR-E007 asks whether stable computational organisation modifies that distance field.

If so, the resulting hierarchy becomes:

\[
\text{computation}
\rightarrow
\text{topology}
\rightarrow
\text{metric}
\rightarrow
\text{metric distortion by persistent structure}.
\]

This is the first step toward a computational analogue of matter influencing geometry.

# 6. What Counts as a Persistent Structure?

A persistent structure is not initially defined as a particle.

It is a computational pattern satisfying a preregistered persistence criterion.

Let:

\[
P_t\subset K_t
\]

represent a local pattern.

Define persistence:

\[
\Pi(P)
=
\frac{
\text{time pattern identity remains recoverable}
}{
\text{observation duration}
}.
\]

A candidate structure must survive:

- local rewrites;
- substrate evolution;
- limited perturbations;
- or replacement of individual constituent simplices.

Persistence therefore concerns pattern identity rather than frozen components.

---

# 7. Pattern Identity

A structure may move or change its internal representation while remaining recognisably the same pattern.

Define an invariant signature:

\[
I(P_t).
\]

Persistence requires:

\[
I(P_t)\approx I(P_{t+\Delta t})
\]

within preregistered tolerance.

Possible signatures include:

- local adjacency pattern;
- simplex-incidence motif;
- state-cycle signature;
- conserved informational label;
- recurring dynamical orbit.

The exact signature must be formalised before confirmatory testing.

---

# 8. No Mass Variable

No structure receives a primitive quantity called:

\[
m.
\]

Instead record measurable computational properties such as:

- persistence duration;
- update frequency;
- internal state count;
- transition density;
- local computational load;
- structural redundancy;
- repair cost;
- information content.

If a mass-like quantity eventually emerges, it must be derived from these variables.

---

# 9. No Position Coordinates

The structure has no:

\[
(x,y,z).
\]

Its location is relational.

At time \(t\), define its support:

\[
S(P_t)
\]

as the set of simplices or dual nodes participating in the persistent pattern.

A relational centre may be defined using graph or cost distance, but not external coordinates.

---

# 10. Baseline Substrate

The preferred baseline is a manifold-like simplicial substrate satisfying E006 criteria:

\[
M_L\approx1
\]

with stable computational metric:

\[
d_C.
\]

This prevents topology failure from being confused with metric distortion.

# 11. E007-A — Static Persistent Structure

The first track freezes the persistent pattern's identity while allowing the surrounding substrate to evolve.

The structure itself does not move.

Question:

> Does maintaining the pattern alter the surrounding cost field?

This isolates metric effects from motion.

---

# 12. E007-B — Dynamical Persistent Structure

The pattern is allowed to propagate relationally while preserving identity.

Question:

> Does the metric distortion move with the structure?

A positive result would suggest that the distortion is associated with the computational pattern rather than a fixed region of the substrate.

---

# 13. E007-C — Multiple Structures

Introduce:

\[
P_1,P_2,\ldots,P_n.
\]

Measure whether metric effects:

- superpose;
- interfere;
- saturate;
- screen;
- amplify.

This track should follow single-structure validation.

# 14. Computational Cost Coupling

The critical experimental variable is how persistent structures affect local transition cost.

The first cost-coupling rule must be simple and non-gravitational.

For example:

\[
c_e
=
c_e^{(0)}
+
\alpha q_e
\]

where:

\[
q_e
\]

measures computational activity caused by maintaining the persistent pattern near edge \(e\).

No distance from the structure appears in the rule.

Only local activity does.

---

# 15. Important Constraint

The cost update must not say:

> increase cost near the particle.

That would encode the desired effect.

Instead:

> cost responds locally to computational activity.

The structure happens to generate that activity.

Thus:

\[
\text{structure}
\rightarrow
\text{local computation}
\rightarrow
\text{cost}
\]

rather than:

\[
\text{structure}
\rightarrow
\text{predefined geometric field}.
\]

---

# 16. Local Computational Load

Define:

\[
q_i(t)
\]

as recent local computational activity around relational node \(i\).

Possible components:

- number of rewrites;
- failed transition attempts;
- state-maintenance updates;
- repair operations;
- information exchanges.

Normalize:

\[
q_i\in[0,1].
\]

For edge \(e=(i,j)\):

\[
q_e=
\frac{q_i+q_j}{2}.
\]

---

# 17. Cost Field

The resulting cost becomes:

\[
c_e(t)
=
1+\alpha q_e(t)
\]

for the simplest baseline.

Alternative registered couplings may later include nonlinear forms:

\[
c_e
=
1+\alpha q_e^\gamma.
\]

But the baseline should remain simple.

---

# 18. Metric Distortion

Given baseline metric:

\[
d_0(A,B),
\]

and metric with structure:

\[
d_P(A,B),
\]

define:

\[
\Delta d(A,B)
=
d_P(A,B)-d_0(A,B).
\]

This is the fundamental E007 observable.

---

# 19. Radial Analysis Without Coordinates

We need a notion analogous to distance from the structure without using Euclidean coordinates.

For any node \(x\), define relational separation from structure:

\[
r_P(x)
=
\min_{p\in S(P)}d_0(x,p).
\]

This uses the baseline computational metric.

Group nodes into shells:

\[
\mathcal{R}_k
=
\{
x:
r_k\leq r_P(x)<r_{k+1}
\}.
\]

This allows field-like behaviour to be measured relationally.

---

# 20. Metric-Distortion Profile

Define mean distortion at shell \(r\):

\[
D(r)
=
E[
\Delta d(x,y)
\mid
x,y\text{ probe shell }r
].
\]

Alternative local observables include cost excess:

\[
\Delta c(r)
=
E[c_e-c_e^{(0)}].
\]

No functional form is assumed.

---

# 21. No Inverse-Square Target

E007 must not attempt to fit:

\[
\frac{1}{r}
\]

or:

\[
\frac{1}{r^2}
\]

as a success criterion.

If such forms appear, they are exploratory observations requiring new confirmation experiments.

The primary test is simply:

\[
D(r)\neq0
\]

in a reproducible structure-dependent way.

---

# 22. Localisation Test

A metric distortion associated with a persistent structure should ideally be spatially local in relational terms.

Test whether:

\[
D(r)
\]

decreases or otherwise systematically changes with relational separation.

A completely uniform global shift may indicate a global resource effect rather than local geometry.

---

# 23. Structure Removal Test

At time:

\[
t_R,
\]

remove the persistent structure while leaving the substrate intact.

Measure whether:

\[
C^{(P)}
\rightarrow
C^{(0)}
\]

and:

\[
d^{(P)}
\rightarrow
d^{(0)}.
\]

This provides a causal intervention.

A structure-associated field should relax when the structure disappears.

---

# 24. Structure Insertion Test

Start from an equilibrated structure-free substrate.

At time:

\[
t_I,
\]

introduce \(P\).

Measure the response:

\[
\Delta C(t)
\]

and:

\[
\Delta d(t).
\]

This allows estimation of field formation time.

---

# 25. Metric Response Time

Define:

\[
\tau_G
\]

as the time for a chosen metric observable to reach a fixed fraction of its steady-state change.

For example:

\[
D(t_I+\tau_G)
=
0.9D_\infty.
\]

This gives the metric field a dynamical timescale.

---

# 26. Metric Relaxation Time

After structure removal, define:

\[
\tau_R
\]

for the metric perturbation to return within tolerance of baseline.

Comparing:

\[
\tau_G
\]

and:

\[
\tau_R
\]

# 27. Hysteresis

If:

\[
C_t
\]

depends on prior activity, removal of a structure may not instantly restore baseline geometry.

This produces computational metric memory.

Measure:

\[
H=
\int
|D_{\text{insertion}}(t)
-
D_{\text{removal}}(t)|
dt.
\]

This is secondary.

---

# 28. Matched Activity Control

A critical control introduces a non-persistent process generating the same average local computational load:

\[
q_e
\]

as the persistent structure.

If both produce identical metric effects, persistence itself may be irrelevant.

The experiment must compare:

\[
P_{\text{persistent}}
\]

with:

\[
P_{\text{activity-matched transient}}.
\]

---

# 29. Random Activity Control

Generate equal total computational activity at random locations.

Match:

\[
\sum_eq_e.
\]

Then compare metric distortion.

This tests whether localisation and persistence matter.

---

# 30. Frozen Cost Control

Generate the same cost profile produced by \(P\), then freeze it and remove the structure.

This distinguishes:

\[
\text{metric effect caused by cost}
\]

from:

\[
\text{additional effects caused by ongoing structure dynamics}.
\]

# 31. Structure-Shuffled Control

Preserve the same local computational components but destroy their coherent pattern identity.

Thus total:

- node count;
- update count;
- local activity

remain similar, while persistence is removed.

Compare metric effects.

# 32. Size-Matched Structure Controls

No relationship is assumed.

# 33. Persistence-Matched Controls

Test whether distortion correlates more strongly with:

This begins to distinguish structural size from computational persistence.

# 34. Computational Burden

\[

Again, this is an observational analysis.

# 35. Candidate Mass-Like Observable

Only after E007 data exists should a candidate computational mass-like quantity be considered.

One possible definition:

\[

# 36. Why This Matters

If metric distortion scales consistently with some intrinsic property of persistent structures, then that property becomes a candidate source quantity.

Conceptually:

\[
\text{persistent computational invariant}
\rightarrow
\text{metric response}.
\]

That resembles the role played by stress-energy in general relativity, but E007 must not make that identification prematurely.

---

# 37. Local Versus Global Cost Conservation

E007 should test two major families.

### Open Cost Model

Additional computational activity increases total cost:

\[
\sum_ec_e
\]

may rise.

### Conserved Cost Model

Total cost remains fixed:

\[
\sum_ec_e=C_{\mathrm{total}}.
\]

Local increases require redistribution elsewhere.

These correspond to very different physical intuitions and should be tested separately.

---

# 38. Conserved-Cost Redistribution

In the conserved model:

\[
\delta c_{\text{near}}>0
\]

requires:

\[
\sum_{\text{far}}\delta c<0.
\]

This creates a metric redistribution problem.

The geometry could become distorted globally even though the initiating computation is local.

---

# 39. Computational Arbitrage Interpretation

The conserved-cost model begins to connect with the CBR concept of computational arbitrage.

If one region accumulates additional computational burden, the surrounding system may redistribute transition opportunities to maintain global constraints.

Then:

\[
\text{local imbalance}
\rightarrow
\text{cost redistribution}
\rightarrow
\text{metric distortion}.
\]

E007 should measure this without yet introducing motion.

# 65. Primary Endpoint

The primary E007 endpoint is:

> **Whether a persistent computational structure causes a reproducible local change in the endogenous computational metric relative to activity-matched, shuffled-pattern and structure-free controls.**

The primary result does not require attraction, motion or any particular field law.

---

# 66. Primary Evidence Conditions

Evidence supporting CBR-H007 requires:

1. persistent structures cause statistically reproducible metric distortion;

2. distortion exceeds matched transient-activity controls;

3. distortion is localised or otherwise systematically related to relational separation;

4. removal or insertion causally changes the metric;

5. the effect survives independent seeds;

6. the effect survives increasing system size;

7. an independent metric implementation reproduces it.

---

# 67. Stronger Evidence Conditions

A stronger result would additionally show:

- distortion follows a moving structure;
- internal observers can detect the distortion;
- multiple structure motifs produce similar responses;
- metric response scales systematically with intrinsic structure properties;
- results survive alternative computational cost rules.

---

# 68. Failure Conditions

CBR-E007 fails to support the hypothesis if:

- metric changes are indistinguishable from activity-matched controls;
- cost-shuffling preserves the effect;
- distortion does not track the persistent structure;
- effects vanish with system size;
- independent implementation fails to reproduce them;
- topology change explains the apparent metric effect.

---

# 69. Particularly Important Negative Result

A critical possible result is:

\[
\text{persistent structure}
\]

has no special metric effect beyond:

\[
\text{equivalent computational load}.
\]

This would imply that persistence itself may not be the relevant source property.

The hypothesis would need refinement.

---

# 70. Another Important Negative Result

The metric may react only at the exact support of the structure:

\[
D(r)=0
\]

for:

\[
r>0.
\]

This would show a local cost effect but no emergent extended field.

That would substantially constrain later gravity interpretations.

---

# 71. Weak Positive Result

A weak positive result is:

> Persistent structures measurably modify local transition cost.

This establishes computational coupling.

---

# 72. Strong Positive Result

A stronger result is:

> The local cost change induces a stable extended metric distortion measurable by embedded probes.

This establishes something field-like.

---

# 73. Strongest E007 Result

The strongest result would combine:

\[
\boxed{
\text{persistent structure}
}
\]

with:

\[
\boxed{
\text{reproducible extended metric distortion}
}
\]

that:

- tracks the structure;
- scales with an intrinsic computational property;
- is approximately isotropic for symmetric structures;
- is operationally measurable internally;
- persists across cost models and implementations.

At that point it becomes reasonable to investigate the structure as a candidate source of emergent curvature.

---

# 74. Interpretation Boundary

Even the strongest E007 result would not establish gravity.

It would establish:

> **Persistent computational organisation can act as a source of emergent metric distortion in a coordinate-free computational manifold.**

There would still be no demonstration that another structure responds to that metric in a gravitationally analogous way.

That is the purpose of E008.
