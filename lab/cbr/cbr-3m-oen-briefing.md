# CBR Open Experimental Network
## One-Page Institutional Briefing
### An Open, Adversarial Research Programme for Testing Compute-Based Reality

**CBR-OEN** is an open scientific collaboration designed to test whether dimensionality, topology, geometry, matter-like persistence, motion and causal structure can emerge from minimal computational systems without being explicitly programmed as target physical properties.

Its purpose is not to prove Compute-Based Reality.

Its purpose is to make it testable.

> **CBR-OEN does not exist to prove Compute-Based Reality. It exists to make Compute-Based Reality testable.**

---

## The Research Question

The programme begins with a deliberately minimal question:

> **Can geometry-free computation generate structures that increasingly resemble the mathematical and physical organisation of our universe?**

Rather than starting with general relativity or a predefined three-dimensional space, the programme progressively tests whether the required structures emerge.

The experimental chain is:

\[
\text{relations}
\rightarrow
\text{dimension}
\rightarrow
\text{3-manifold topology}
\rightarrow
\text{metric}
\rightarrow
\text{persistent structures}
\rightarrow
\text{metric distortion}
\rightarrow
\text{geodesic motion}
\rightarrow
\text{causality}
\rightarrow
\text{quantitative gravity}.
\]

---

## Ten Foundational Experiments

**CBR-E001 — Emergent Dimensionality**  
Can effective dimension emerge from relational computation with no coordinates or target dimension?

**CBR-E002 — Dimension-Blind Evolution**  
Does selection for persistence, complexity and stability organise dimensionality even though dimension is absent from fitness?

**CBR-E003 — 3-Manifold Emergence**  
Do systems with approximately three-dimensional scaling also possess local topology compatible with a 3-manifold?

**CBR-E004 — Explicit Simplicial Dynamics**  
Can local computation over tetrahedra maintain evolving 3-manifold topology without background coordinates?

**CBR-E005 — Manifold Resilience**  
If topology-breaking defects are permitted, does topology-blind selection favour avoidance, resilience or repair?

**CBR-E006 — Emergent Metric**  
Can minimum computational transition cost become operational distance?

**CBR-E007 — Structure–Metric Coupling**  
Can persistent computational structures systematically distort that metric?

**CBR-E008 — Emergent Geodesic Motion**  
Do free computational structures follow the resulting geometry without an explicit attractive force?

**CBR-E009 — Emergent Causality**  
Can local computation produce stable causal cones and an approximately universal limiting propagation speed?

**CBR-E010 — Quantitative Correspondence**  
Can one emergent geometry quantitatively predict multiple weak-field gravitational phenomena using a common parameter set?

## Experimental Discipline

The programme is specifically designed to avoid constructing the desired result into the model.

Examples include:

- no spatial coordinates in the foundational experiments;
- no target \(d=3\) in evolutionary fitness;
- no manifold score in topology-blind selection;
- no gravitational force in free-motion experiments;
- no Lorentz transform in causal experiments;
- no inverse-square rule in the quantitative gravity experiment.

The general principle is:

> **Do not ask the computational system to produce the physical property we hope to explain. Ask more primitive questions and measure what appears without being requested.**

## Independent Roles

CBR-OEN separates major scientific functions wherever practical:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Propose}
\rightarrow
\text{Implement}
\rightarrow
\text{Execute}
\rightarrow
\text{Analyse}
\rightarrow
\text{Replicate}
\rightarrow
\text{Challenge}
}
\]

Participating institutions may contribute as:

- hypothesis groups;
- mathematics and topology groups;
- independent implementation teams;
- simulation and HPC nodes;
- statistical analysis groups;
- relativity and physics groups;
- adversarial review groups;
- replication centres.

Participation does **not** imply endorsement of CBR.

## Adversarial Collaboration

Major experiments should ideally involve:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Proponent}
+
\text{Sceptic}
+
\text{Verifier}.
}
\]

The sceptic participates before execution and helps define:

- alternative explanations;
- controls;
- failure conditions;
- falsification criteria.

Negative results are published as first-class scientific outputs.

---

## Reproducibility Standard

CBR-OEN uses three levels:

**R1 — Computational reproduction**  
Same implementation and seed reproduce the result.

**R2 — Independent implementation reproduction**  
A separately written implementation reproduces the phenomenon.

**R3 — Institutional replication**  
An independent organisation reproduces the preregistered finding.

Strong physical claims are expected to reach R3.

---

## The Experiment Is the Specification

CBR-OEN experiments are defined by machine-readable specifications rather than by one software implementation.

A specification records:

- hypothesis;
- null hypothesis;
- allowed computational rules;
- prohibited assumptions;
- measurements;
- sample sizes;
- randomness;
- controls;
- analysis plan;
- stopping criteria;
- failure conditions.

> **CBR-Lab or any future implementation is not the experiment. The specification is the experiment.**

This enables independent implementations in Python, Julia, Rust, C++, Mathematica or other environments.

## What Would Count as Success?

CBR-OEN does not define success as proving CBR.

Scientific success includes demonstrating rigorously that:

- dimensionality does not emerge;
- manifold topology does not emerge;
- metric geometry does not arise;
- matter-like structures do not alter geometry;
- emergent geometry does not determine motion;
- relativistic causality does not appear;
- gravity-like behaviour fails quantitative correspondence.

A rigorous falsification is a successful outcome for the network.

---

## What Would Be a Strong Positive Result?

The strongest first-programme result would be a chain in which:

\[
\text{minimal local computation}
\]

produces independently:

\[
\text{3-manifold topology},
\]

\[
\text{operational metric geometry},
\]

\[
\text{persistent structures},
\]

\[
\text{metric distortion},
\]

\[
\text{geodesic-like motion},
\]

\[
\text{causal structure},
\]

and finally:

\[
\boxed{
\text{quantitative predictions of previously unseen simulation outcomes}.
}
\]

Only then would comparison with physical reality become especially compelling.

## Invitation to Participate

CBR-OEN invites mathematics, physics, computer-science, complexity-science and computational research institutions to:

- critique the hypotheses;
- strengthen the mathematics;
- implement experiments independently;
- contribute compute;
- analyse blinded datasets;
- attempt replication;
- identify confounders;
- design falsification tests;
- publish negative findings;
- propose competing explanations.

No participant is required to accept the theoretical interpretation.

The shared objective is simply:

> **Where a claim about the computational foundations of reality can be converted into a reproducible mathematical or computational experiment, test it openly, independently and adversarially.**

---

## Founding Commitment

If CBR fails, the network should tell us why.

If it survives, the network should tell us how strongly.

That is the purpose of the **CBR Open Experimental Network**.
